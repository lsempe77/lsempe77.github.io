§1 · The problem

Systematic reviews are slow, expensive, and the AI tools that promise to fix them aren't trustworthy.


  • 1.Reviews take too long. A Cochrane review averages 27 months from protocol to publication. A 3ie SR or REA is shorter but still months of labour-intensive work.
  • 2.Existing tools are too thin. Screening-focused tools stop at title/abstract triage; a bolt-on ML classifier doesn't model GRADE as five separate factors, handle network meta-analysis, or produce a PRISMA 2020–compliant report bundle from the same data store.
  • 3.LLM tools proliferate but can't be trusted. A bare ChatGPT prompt will screen abstracts — but with no calibration, no provenance, no way to show a journal editor or funder that the AI was actually reliable for this review.
  • 4.Nothing is calibrated to 3ie's own practice. 3ie runs full SRs, Rapid Evidence Assessments, Evidence & Gap Maps, and scoping reviews — with its own databases, its own extraction taxonomy, and its own house reporting template. Generic tools assume one format.
RAISE 2 (2026) — the international standard for building and evaluating AI evidence-synthesis tools — explicitly criticises SR platforms that lack "publicly available source code" and "published performance evaluations." The field's own standard-setters have named the gap this platform is built to close. Responsible AI use in Systematic Evidence Synthesis, OSF osf.io/fwaud, draft v3.1, March 2026

The Cochrane Rapid Reviews Methods Group (2025)

States that AI in rapid reviews should serve as quality assurance — catching errors human single-reviewer workflows miss — not as a fast path that relaxes oversight. The platform's design reflects exactly this: AI proposes, human confirms.

§1 · What the right platform requires

Seven requirements a rigorous evidence-synthesis platform cannot skip.

Not a feature race against any one product — a normative bar drawn from the Cochrane Handbook, PRISMA 2020, GRADE and RAISE. This is what "done right" looks like, and what this platform is built to satisfy.

Plural risk-of-bias instruments

RCTs, non-randomised studies, diagnostic-accuracy studies and reviews-of-reviews each need a different appraisal tool. Seven pluggable instruments here — RoB 2, ROBINS-I/E, QUADAS-2, AMSTAR-2, JBI, NOS — not one bolted onto everything.

GRADE as five separate factors

Risk of bias, inconsistency, indirectness, imprecision and publication bias are five distinct judgement calls, not one text field. Each is its own database column, with its own narrative and confirmation gate.

AI measured, not asserted

A screening or extraction model is only as trustworthy as its last calibration run. Every AI step here is piloted against a human gold batch first — recall, precision, WSS@95 and κ are stored, not claimed.

Tamper-evident provenance

Every state-changing write and every AI call needs a verifiable record. Both are hash-chained here from day one — append-only, and tamper detection is structural, not a promise.

Full PRISMA 2020 compliance

Flow-diagram counts alone are not compliance. The full 27-item checklist is implemented here, with four items auto-detected straight from structured data rather than filled in by hand after the fact.

First-class review types

A rapid evidence assessment, an Evidence & Gap Map and a full systematic review are methodologically different products. review_type is a first-class, cascading field here, not a label on an otherwise identical workflow.

Open, verifiable methodology

Calibration metrics and the audit trail should be inspectable, not asserted on reputation. Every metric this platform reports is a database row with provenance behind it — publishable on request.

§3 · Stage 3

Screening — two passes, layered AI reliability, a statistically-grounded stopping rule.

✅ Built — live in the backend today, not a mockup
  • ◆Two PRISMA stages: title/abstract and full-text share one verdict model but different rules — full-text exclusions require a reason_code pointing to a live eligibility criterion (PRISMA mandate). Title/abstract doesn't.
  • ◆Three AI reliability layers: (1) stability — the same input is scored k times; agreement below threshold → abstain; (2) critic pass — a second independent model re-examines; disagreement → abstain; (3) debate mode — include-advocate → exclude-advocate → synthesiser rules on both. Abstained items always route to a human.
  • ◆Relevance-ranked queue + buscar stopping estimator (hypergeometric p-value for "true recall below target"). When p ≤ 0.05 the platform flags "≈95% of includes found" — a statistically-grounded claim, not a guess.
  • ◆Span-anchored full-text AI: the model is shown up to 12,000 characters of the parsed PDF and returns page-anchored evidence_anchors — verifying an AI verdict means reading the cited excerpt, not re-reading the whole PDF.
  • ◆Dual review: assignments (random/stratified/dual/triple), conflict detection, AI-as-arbitrator with 20% human spot-check, Cohen's κ / Fleiss' κ / PABAK / percent agreement.
External benchmark (Zhan et al., 2026): naive single-pass full-text LLM screening reached 97.6% sensitivity / only 47.4% specificity (72.5% balanced accuracy). That's the baseline a simple "add AI" approach delivers. The stability+critic+debate stack is a justified investment against exactly that failure mode. Zhan et al., Systematic Reviews (2026)

§3 · Stage 6 & 7
✅ Built — both live in the backend today, not a mockup

Synthesis

  • ◆Pairwise meta-analysis via a dedicated R statistics engine: REML+HKSJ pooling (Cochrane default), I²/H²/τ²/Q + prediction interval, 8 τ² estimators, publication-bias diagnostics, subgroup, meta-regression — matched against a metafor reference run.

§4 · AI reliability

Not "we added AI." We measure it, chain it, and make it provable.


Every call is hash-chained

Model id, version, seed, input hash, timestamp — stored on a tamper-evident, append-only provenance chain. Every AI-assisted decision is traceable to the exact model state that produced it. A future auditor can verify the chain without attaching a debugger.

Measure, don't assert

Before any AI step runs at scale, it is calibrated against a human gold batch. Recall, precision, WSS@95, and Cohen's κ are stored on the chain — not claimed in prose. A tool that has been calibrated and publishes the numbers can make claims many competing tools currently can only assert in marketing copy.

AI proposes, human confirms

No AI verdict, extraction value, or RoB domain rating auto-applies without a human confirmation event. The confirmed_by + confirmed_at stamps are the auditable record. RAISE 1 §1.8's disclosure test is met by construction, not by assertion.

Layer 1 — Abstention

Confidence below 0.7, or multi-run agreement below threshold → abstain → route to human. Never a silent guess.

Layer 2 — Stability

Same prompt, k runs at temperature 0 then seeded 0.4. prob_stdev and agreement stored per result. Unstable → abstain.

Layer 3 — Critic pass

A second, independent model re-examines the primary verdict. Disagreement → abstain → human. Runs after the stability check, so dead items waste no critic call.

Layer 4 — Debate mode

For conflicts: include-advocate argues for inclusion, exclude-advocate argues for exclusion, a synthesiser rules on both arguments. Three sequential LLM calls, all provenance-stamped.

§6 · What it will look like

A proposed design direction — a full mockup for every stage.

The interface is designed like a scientific instrument — dense but calm, keyboard-first, and honest about what the AI did, with the provenance chain always one glance away.

Palette — paper + ink + one accent

All colours are OKLCH semantic tokens (--paper, --ink, --accent oklch(0.470 0.135 264)). Dark mode is a token swap, not per-component overrides. The Cochrane RoB traffic-light colours (good/warn/bad) are held strictly separate from the accent.

Typography — DM Sans + DM Mono

DM Sans (variable optical size axis) for all UI text; DM Mono for field keys, confidence scores, model ids, hashes. Chosen for legibility at the dense 13–15px range where the product lives.
